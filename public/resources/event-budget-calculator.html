Our pre-commit benchmark (not the capital campaign number)

You may have seen a 50% or higher pre-commit target cited elsewhere. That number comes from capital campaigns, a different kind of fundraising built on an 18 to 24 month cultivation cycle run by major-gift staff toward a handful of very large lead gifts. It isn't a benchmark for a single night's paddle raise.

Tell us a bit about your event, and we'll show EPA's benchmark for it:

You should have 20% to 30% of your paddle raise goal pre-committed before the event begins. For your event, that's about $0 to $0. Securing these gifts in advance, often called planting major gifts, establishes critical momentum, builds social proof, and prevents an awkward silence during your highest tier asks.

Pre-Commitment Breakdown

  • Top Tiers. Lock in your highest giving levels (your $10,000, $5,000, or $2,500 brackets, for example) with major donors, board members, or close supporters ahead of time.
  • The Anchor. Secure at least one substantial gift at your very top tier before guests walk into the room. This is the gift that sets the tone for the rest of the ask.
  • The Room's Role. Leave the rest to be raised organically in the room, as the energy builds and the mid to lower tier paddles start going up.

A note on bigger numbers: some sources cite 40% or more pre-committed before the event. That figure reflects capital campaign practice, not a benefit auction, and it takes a dedicated major-gift team working the phones for months, not a three-week call list. Very few one-night paddle raises get there, and they don't need to.
